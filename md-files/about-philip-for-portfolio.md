# About Philip — Portfolio Reference

Pulled from the Philip Content OS project, trimmed to just what's useful for building/writing the portfolio site. Paste this into your new project (as a doc, or into project instructions) as a starting point — edit anything that doesn't fit a job-hunting context.

## Who you are (brand core)

Danish creator and video editor, 22, focused on short-form content, editing, and cinematography — someone who understands *how content works*, not just how software works. Three things make the brand:

- **Skills** — real editing craft, not just tool knowledge
- **Taste** — a recognisable visual point of view (cinematography, colour, sound, pacing, storytelling)
- **Person** — someone people want to follow for the process, not just isolated tips

For the portfolio specifically, the framing that matters most: *not just another 22-year-old who's opened Premiere Pro once* — you have real output, real results, and a personal brand that proves you understand content beyond the software.

## The full chain you understand

```
idea → hook → capture → structure → edit → sound → colour → publish → learn
```

Worth stating explicitly on the site — most junior editors only own the "edit" step. You own the whole chain.

## Tone of voice (use this when writing bio copy, captions, or any first-person text)

- conversational, direct, confident without arrogance
- warm but not "bestie" energy
- casual but precise — understate rather than exaggerate
- honest about uncertainty/trade-offs where relevant
- short paragraphs, gets to the point fast
- avoid guru language, hype words, ALL CAPS claims, exaggerated certainty

**Avoid:** "This changed EVERYTHING", "3 secrets nobody tells you", "you're doing this completely wrong" — anything that sounds like a template or a guru script.

**Aim for:** something you'd actually say out loud. Proof over adjectives — a number or a named result beats "passionate" or "creative" every time.

## Visual identity (from the content brand — optional reference, not a rule for the site)

- black/dark-grey base, white type
- yellow accent `#FFDB00`
- headline font: Anton · caption font: PP Editorial New · body/carousel font: Neue Montreal
- premium/editorial feel, never a generic template look

The portfolio site I built uses its own palette (near-black + lime accent) rather than this exact one — reuse the content-brand colors only if you want tight visual continuity between your Instagram/TikTok presence and the site. Otherwise the site's current identity works fine standalone.

## Audience shift: content brand vs. portfolio site

Important distinction — the Content OS brand guide is written for growing a Danish audience on social media. The portfolio site has a **different, narrower job**: convincing a hiring manager or brand you're worth booking/hiring. That means:

- primary audience here = recruiters / hiring managers / brand marketing leads, not Danish creator-community followers
- English is probably the safer default language for the site (recruiters at brands may not read Danish) — flag if you're specifically targeting Danish companies
- content should lean more concrete/professional than a casual Creator Thoughts post would — proof, numbers, named projects — while keeping your actual voice

## Suggested lightweight structure for the new project

Nowhere near the 15-doc setup of Content OS — just enough for continuity:

1. **About Philip (light)** — this doc, refined by you
2. **Portfolio content tracker** — running list of real projects/videos as you finish them: title, brand/client, link, result/stat, what to say about it
3. (optional) **Site copy drafts** — hero line, about paragraph, etc. as you iterate on wording

That's enough memory for me to keep writing/adjusting the site in your voice without re-explaining yourself each session — without dragging in the trend-radar workflow that doesn't apply here.
